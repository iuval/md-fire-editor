Refund policy

Getting your money back

You can ask for a refund of unused credit within 14 days of buying it. This page explains what counts as unused, how to ask, and what happens next.

Last updated 23 November 2026.

The 14 day window

A top-up can be refunded in full if you ask within 14 days of the purchase and none of that credit has been spent. Credit is spent as you use the service above the free daily quota, at the per-unit prices on the pricing page.

If part of the credit has been spent, we refund what is left of that purchase, in whole cents. After 14 days the credit is no longer refundable, but it stays usable for 24 months from the purchase date.

How to ask

Write to iuvalgoldansky@gmail.com from the email address of your FireMD account, and include the receipt or order number that Polar sent you. If you do not have it, tell us the date and amount and we will find the order.

You do not need to give a reason. Asking is enough.

What is refundable

Credit bought in the last 14 days, unspent Refunded in full.
Credit bought in the last 14 days, partly spent Refunded for the unspent remainder.
Credit bought more than 14 days ago Not refundable, still usable until it expires.
Duplicate or unauthorised charge Refunded in full, whenever we find it.
Paid feature not delivered, or a long outage Refunded in full or credited, whichever you prefer.
The free quota Nothing was paid, so nothing to refund.

What happens next

We answer within 5 business days. Approved refunds are issued by Polar, our merchant of record, to the original payment method, with the corresponding tax returned. The money reaches your card or bank on your provider's timeline, usually 5 to 10 business days after the refund is issued.

Refunds are processed through Polar because it is the seller on record. You will receive its confirmation, and the refund appears on the same statement as the original charge.

Before a chargeback

If a charge looks wrong, write to us first. We can usually refund it faster than a bank dispute, and a chargeback during an open conversation only delays the money.

Your statutory rights

Nothing on this page reduces rights you have under mandatory consumer law where you live, including withdrawal rights that may apply in your jurisdiction. If the two conflict, the law wins and we apply it.
